# Mission GroundWork — Commercial Frontier Patch

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Status:** Research-backed go-to-market patch; not yet a public offer or performance claim.

## Source boundary

This patch follows standalone review of current platform, government, and sector materials. It adds only opportunities with a direct fit to Mission GroundWork’s existing promise: helping trusted organizations make their work findable, understandable, usable, and easier to act on.

It does not claim that Mission GroundWork has already delivered these services, that client outcomes are guaranteed, or that automated tools establish compliance.

---

# Opportunity 1 — Generative Search Evidence Baseline

- **Customer:** Nonprofits, churches, community organizations, museums, archives, and small service organizations with useful public content.
- **Pain:** The organization cannot tell whether its pages appear in AI Overviews, AI Mode, or other generative discovery surfaces, and may be reacting to generic “GEO” marketing without evidence.
- **Current solution:** Conventional SEO audit, Google Search Console, traffic reports, or an AEO/GEO consultant.
- **Why current solutions are insufficient:** Search Console is beginning to expose dedicated generative-AI visibility, while many commercial audits still rely on manual searches, screenshots, or proprietary scores with unclear validity.
- **Market timing:** `EARLY TRACTION`
- **Evidence of traction:** Google launched limited dedicated generative-search reporting in Search Console and published explicit AI-feature guidance for site owners.
- **Evidence against:** The reports remain limited, impressions do not equal visits or conversions, and Google does not promise inclusion.
- **Likely business model:** Fixed-fee baseline plus optional quarterly evidence review.
- **Likely acquisition channel:** Existing website audit outreach, nonprofit networks, museum/archive networks, and trusted referrals.
- **Revenue path:** Paid audit → prioritized content/technical corrections → optional ongoing evidence review.
- **Delivery burden:** Moderate; requires Search Console access, content inspection, conventional SEO checks, and interpretation without invented causality.
- **Capital and skill requirements:** Low capital; high research, content, technical SEO, and evidence-communication skill.
- **Speed to first sale:** Short if attached to an existing website or communications audit.
- **Defensibility:** Clear before/after evidence, sector-specific interpretation, and integration with Trust-to-Action communications.
- **Commoditization risk:** High if sold as generic “AI SEO.”
- **Why now:** The measurement surface has started to exist.
- **Why it may be too late:** SEO agencies are rapidly relabeling services as AEO/GEO.
- **Smallest credible market test:** Produce one evidence baseline for a site with verified Search Console access; do not promise ranking improvement.
- **Recommended action:** `VALIDATE WITH CUSTOMERS`

### Service-language boundary

Use language such as:

> We establish what can currently be observed about your visibility in Google’s AI search experiences, identify content and technical gaps, and separate evidence from speculation.

Do not promise “ranking in ChatGPT,” guaranteed citations, or universal AI visibility.

# Opportunity 2 — Responsible AI GroundWork Review

- **Customer:** Nonprofits already using generative AI informally across fundraising, communications, program work, administration, or research.
- **Pain:** AI is present but invisible: staff use different tools, sensitive information may be entered without agreement, and leaders cannot distinguish useful workflows from reputational or operational risk.
- **Current solution:** Generic AI policy templates, prompt workshops, vendor demonstrations, or no coordinated approach.
- **Why current solutions are insufficient:** A policy without workflow evidence can become symbolic; prompt instruction does not resolve data handling, review responsibility, tool selection, or outcome measurement.
- **Market timing:** `GROWING`
- **Evidence of traction:** Candid and TechSoup are publishing current nonprofit AI policy and adoption guidance; sector research describes a gap between ad hoc use and mature, responsible practice.
- **Evidence against:** Survey associations do not prove that AI maturity causes fundraising or retention gains, and many organizations may lack budget or urgency.
- **Likely business model:** Fixed-fee diagnostic with an AI-use inventory, risk map, decision rules, and 90-day bounded pilot recommendations.
- **Likely acquisition channel:** Executive-director networks, funder capacity-building programs, nonprofit associations, and current consulting relationships.
- **Revenue path:** Diagnostic → policy/workflow repair → staff implementation support → evidence review.
- **Delivery burden:** Moderate to high; requires interviews, workflow evidence, privacy boundaries, and role-specific recommendations.
- **Capital and skill requirements:** Low capital; high facilitation, nonprofit operations, governance, and AI-literacy requirements.
- **Speed to first sale:** Moderate.
- **Defensibility:** Sector context, evidence discipline, human-centered implementation, and integration with actual organizational work.
- **Commoditization risk:** High for generic policy templates; lower for evidence-grounded operating reviews.
- **Why now:** Organizations are moving beyond individual experimentation but often lack a decision layer.
- **Why it may be too late:** Large software vendors and consulting firms are packaging responsible-AI services.
- **Smallest credible market test:** One 60–90 minute leadership interview, anonymous staff use inventory, and a bounded risk/opportunity memo.
- **Recommended action:** `INVESTIGATE IMMEDIATELY`

# Opportunity 3 — AI-Ready Knowledge Base Assessment

- **Customer:** Research-heavy nonprofits, museums, archives, associations, public-interest institutes, and organizations with extensive policies, reports, program guidance, or historical collections.
- **Pain:** General-purpose AI gives incomplete or inaccurate answers about the organization’s own evidence because source material is fragmented, duplicated, inaccessible, stale, or poorly structured.
- **Current solution:** Shared drives, website search, a chatbot added over existing documents, or manual staff retrieval.
- **Why current solutions are insufficient:** A chatbot cannot compensate reliably for weak source architecture, missing provenance, contradictory versions, and no evaluation set.
- **Market timing:** `EARLY TRACTION`
- **Evidence of traction:** Urban Institute is testing custom AI systems built on an AI-ready knowledge base after finding that general-purpose tools struggle with its data.
- **Evidence against:** The preparation and maintenance burden can exceed the value for small or rarely consulted collections.
- **Likely business model:** Assessment and source-map engagement; optional documentation cleanup and evaluation design.
- **Likely acquisition channel:** Archives, museums, research nonprofits, associations, and organizations preparing internal AI assistants.
- **Revenue path:** Evidence inventory → source-of-truth map → readiness score → bounded remediation or implementation partner handoff.
- **Delivery burden:** High; requires content inventory, provenance, version control, permissions, information architecture, and test questions.
- **Capital and skill requirements:** Low software capital for assessment; high knowledge-management and research skill.
- **Speed to first sale:** Moderate.
- **Defensibility:** Domain interpretation and a verifiable evidence architecture rather than a generic chatbot.
- **Commoditization risk:** Moderate.
- **Why now:** Organizations are trying AI assistants and discovering that their knowledge base is the bottleneck.
- **Why it may be too late:** Enterprise search and RAG vendors increasingly bundle readiness services.
- **Smallest credible market test:** Assess one bounded collection of 25–100 documents using ten real staff questions and a provenance check.
- **Recommended action:** `VALIDATE WITH CUSTOMERS`

# Opportunity 4 — Public-Service Accessibility Evidence Audit

- **Customer:** State and local public entities, contractors, grantees, cultural institutions, and community organizations whose web or mobile content supports public programs.
- **Pain:** Teams may not know which pages, documents, forms, third-party services, or mobile functions fall inside accessibility obligations, and may mistake an automated score for compliance.
- **Current solution:** Automated accessibility scanner, website redesign, legal review, or no formal inventory.
- **Why current solutions are insufficient:** Automated testing misses important human-use failures; compliance can involve content delivered through contracts, licensing, and third-party arrangements.
- **Market timing:** `GROWING`
- **Evidence of traction:** The DOJ Title II rule began applying to larger public entities on April 24, 2026 and requires WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA subject to stated exceptions and defenses.
- **Evidence against:** Mission GroundWork is not a law firm; legal interpretation and formal compliance opinions require qualified counsel.
- **Likely business model:** Evidence inventory and remediation coordination, explicitly excluding legal certification.
- **Likely acquisition channel:** Public-sector partners, community organizations, museums, libraries, local agencies, and website referrals.
- **Revenue path:** Inventory → prioritized remediation list → content/design repair → retest and evidence packet.
- **Delivery burden:** Moderate to high; includes automated tests, manual checks, documents, forms, media, and third-party surfaces.
- **Capital and skill requirements:** Low capital; strong accessibility, content, design, and documentation skill.
- **Speed to first sale:** Short where deadlines or procurement requirements are active.
- **Defensibility:** Human-centered evidence, plain-language remediation, and coordination across content and technology.
- **Commoditization risk:** Automated scanning is commoditized; interpreted evidence and remediation remain less so.
- **Why now:** The first major federal compliance date has passed.
- **Why it may be too late:** Established accessibility firms and legal vendors already serve the market.
- **Smallest credible market test:** One public-facing site and its five highest-consequence user paths; label the output an evidence audit, not a compliance certification.
- **Recommended action:** `INVESTIGATE IMMEDIATELY`

# Go-to-market channel implications

## Website and landing pages

Add structured evidence to future service pages:

1. Exact object reviewed
2. What was observed
3. What could not be verified
4. Consequence for the organization and its users
5. Bounded corrective action
6. Evidence produced after correction

## Outreach

Lead with a verifiable observation, not a trend claim. Examples:

- A public service path has inaccessible form controls or unlabeled navigation.
- Search Console has no usable baseline for generative-search visibility.
- Staff are using AI but the organization has no shared rule for sensitive information or review responsibility.
- An internal assistant is being considered while the source collection has contradictory or unowned versions.

## Proof assets

Create proof only after a real engagement or controlled pilot:

- redacted before/after evidence packet;
- source-of-truth map;
- AI-use inventory;
- accessibility user-path report;
- generative-search baseline;
- evaluation-question set.

## Do not publish yet

Do not add these as public offers until each has at least one controlled delivery test, a defined exclusion statement, and an acceptance checklist.
